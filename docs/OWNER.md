# The Owner

Who the agent works for, and how they like to work. Prefilled for Alonso (GitHub `iturbe`) from five earlier projects; edit at kickoff if anything differs for this project. This is a working profile, not a biography. Keep it short and actionable.

## Who

- An engineer and product manager. Comfortable with Python and general software engineering.
- Often new to the specific platform of a project (it was UEFN and Verse in Battle Drill and Shockwave Playground). **Explain platform and editor steps as if to a first-time user:** exact menus, exact button labels.
- Plays and judges the product themselves, and bluntly ("the climb is too hard"). Expects the design to change in response, not to be defended.

## How they like to work

- **Minimum oversight.** Work autonomously. Batch everything that needs them into one place (the human channel) and keep building other parts while waiting: "keep on building the other systems as well".
- **Answers in batches.** Prefers a round of questions answered in one sitting, with a recommended answer first, over questions scattered through chat. Short-list replies work well: `1: yes · 2: option B · 3: …`.
- **Clear test runs.** "Be very clear on what things I need to do in each test run so I do not miss them." Give numbered steps and a checklist that matches them. Name the exact on-screen labels and the build number to expect. Ask for numbers and yes/no answers instead of impressions.
- **Says who and where.** Always state who runs a session (the owner, or an automated robot with nobody touching anything) and on which device.
- **Conclusion first.** Status reports open with where things stand: what is ready, what is blocked, what is waiting on them.
- **The repo is the memory.** A fresh session must continue without the chat. Plans and decisions live in files; a hand-off document is mirrored in the repo.
- **Plain language.** Short sentences, no jargon without a definition, no padding.

## Devices and Accounts

- **Machines:** a MacBook Air (iOS and Python work), Windows build machines (cloud VMs and a Shadow PC for UEFN), and Claude Code cloud sessions. Projects move between machines, so every repo keeps a new-machine checklist.
- **Test devices:** iPhone and iPad (iOS apps), PS5 and Switch 2 (Fortnite islands).
- **GitHub:** `iturbe`. Commits use a GitHub no-reply address, never a personal email.
- **Google Drive and Docs:** used for hand-off documents in iteration mode.
- **Model and media APIs:** Anthropic, ElevenLabs and others, with keys in `.env`. Budget-conscious: Timeline Writer runs at $20–100 a month with a cap enforced in the app.

## Always the Owner's

Sign-ins, 2FA, credentials and sudo. Creating projects in vendor editors and changing their settings. Anything inside a running game client or on a physical device. Publishing, store submissions and account settings. Spending beyond the agreed budget. Deciding scope; judging fun, feel, clarity and taste; canon in creative projects.
